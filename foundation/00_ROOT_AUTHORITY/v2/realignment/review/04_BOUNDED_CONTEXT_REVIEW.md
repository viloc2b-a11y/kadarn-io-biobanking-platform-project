# KADARN v2 — Bounded Context Review

**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Challenge:** Are the 9 proposed bounded contexts truly independent? Each must have its own language, rules, and reason to exist separately.

---

## 9 Proposed Contexts (from Constitution §13)

1. Identity Registry
2. Source Intelligence
3. Evidence & Provenance
4. Claims & Knowledge
5. Capability Intelligence
6. Protocol Intelligence
7. Assessment Engine
8. Publication & Sharing
9. Governance & Audit

---

## Analysis

### Identity Registry vs. Everyone Else

**Claim to independence:** Institution/Person/Location/Membership/Role have their own lifecycle and rules. They don't depend on claims or evidence.

**Verdict: KEEP.** Weak coupling to other contexts (only via foreign keys). Clear boundary.

**Simplification:** None needed. This is the simplest context.

### Source Intelligence vs. Evidence & Provenance

**Claim to independence:** Sources have acquisition rules, freshness policies, authority levels — these are different from evidence normalization.

**Challenge:** Evidence is always derived FROM a source. A source without evidence is metadata. Evidence without a source has no provenance. These two contexts have the strongest coupling in the entire model.

**Red Team question:** Does separating Sources from Evidence create real value? Or does it add JOINs and migration coordination without benefit?

**Verdict: MERGE → "Source & Evidence Intelligence."** The boundary between "source acquisition" and "evidence normalization" is an implementation concern, not a domain boundary. Extraction bridges them — and extraction is a service, not a context.

**Impact:** Reduces 9 → 8 contexts. Simplifies API: evidence routes can return source data without cross-context queries.

### Evidence & Provenance vs. Claims & Knowledge

**Claim to independence:** Evidence is raw; Claims are assertions. Different rules.

**Challenge:** The Constitution says "Claims over Forms" and "Contradictions are Data." Claims exist because of evidence. An orphan claim (no evidence) is just a form submission. The relationship is so tight that separating them forces cross-context queries on every read.

**Verdict: MERGE → "Evidence, Claims & Knowledge."** Evidence exists TO support/contradict claims. Separation creates artificial boundaries that every operation crosses.

**Impact:** Reduces 8 → 7 contexts. Makes "explain confidence" a single-context query instead of cross-context.

### Claims & Knowledge vs. Capability Intelligence

**Claim to independence:** Capabilities are composed from claims. They have their own lifecycle (active → constrained → unavailable).

**Challenge:** A capability IS a set of claims. `CapabilityCompositionService` is a rule engine over claim data. When claims change, capabilities may change. This is not a separate context — it's a service WITHIN Claims & Knowledge.

**Verdict: MERGE into "Claims & Knowledge".** Capability composition is a domain service, not a bounded context. The rules for composing capabilities belong with the claims they compose.

**Impact:** Reduces 7 → 6 contexts. Keeps the compose() logic co-located with claim data.

### Protocol Intelligence vs. Assessment Engine

**Claim to independence:** Protocols are authored data. Assessments are execution records using that data.

**Challenge:** An assessment without a protocol is meaningless. A protocol without assessment capability is just a document. They exist for each other.

**Verdict: MERGE → "Protocol Assessment."** A single context that owns: protocols, versions, requirements, assessments, results, gaps, mitigations. The requirement model and the assessment model are views of the same domain.

**Impact:** Reduces 6 → 5 contexts. Assessment reads requirements without cross-context calls.

### Assessment Engine vs. Publication & Sharing

**Claim to independence:** Publication happens after assessment. Different lifecycle.

**Verdict: KEEP SEPARATE.** Assessments produce snapshots. Publication consumes snapshots. The outputs of one are inputs to the other — this is a clean pipeline boundary, not an artificial split.

### Governance & Audit

**Claim to independence:** Cross-cutting concern. Every context needs audit.

**Verdict: KEEP as shared infrastructure, not a bounded context.** Audit events are generated BY all contexts. A separate "governance context" adds ceremony without value. Implement as a shared audit_events table with an append-only API.

## Final Contexts (5)

| # | Context | Owner | Key Entities |
|---|---------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | **Identity Registry** | Identity | Institution, Person, Location, Membership, Role |
| 2 | **Source & Evidence Intelligence** | Sources + Evidence | EvidenceSource, SourceRecord, Evidence |
| 3 | **Claims, Knowledge & Capability** | Claims | Claim, Capability, Review, EvidenceLink, Review |
| 4 | **Protocol Assessment** | Assessment | Protocol, ProtocolVersion, Requirement, Assessment |
| 5 | **Publication & Sharing** | Publication | KnowledgeSnapshot, Passport, Package, ShareGrant |

**Reduction: 9 → 5 (44% fewer contexts)**
